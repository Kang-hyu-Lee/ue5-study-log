1. A right triangle has legs of length 6 and 8. Find the hypotenuse.

2. A right triangle has a hypotenuse of 13 and one leg of length 5. Find the other leg.
   *(hint: rearrange a² + b² = c² to solve for a leg instead of the hypotenuse — this is algebra applied to the theorem, same move as the worked example in Concepts Section 3)*

3. Right triangle: angle θ = 45°, hypotenuse = 10. Find the length of the side opposite θ.

4. Right triangle: angle θ = 60°, hypotenuse = 8. Find the length of the side adjacent to θ.
   *(hint: which SOH-CAH-TOA ratio relates Adjacent and Hypotenuse? See Concepts Section 4 table)*

5. Right triangle: angle θ = 30°, adjacent side = 20. Find the hypotenuse.
   *(hint: same 3-step process as the worked example, just solving for a different unknown — set up the ratio, plug in, solve algebraically)*

6. In your own words (1-2 sentences): why does "opposite" and "adjacent" depend on which angle you pick as θ, but "hypotenuse" never changes?
