DAY 22 — RATE OF CHANGE EXERCISES
Work by hand. No calculator needed except for arithmetic checks.

1. An object moves from x=0m to x=12m over 3 seconds. Find average velocity.

   12m/3s = 4 m/s

2. An object moves from x=50m to x=20m over 5 seconds. Find average velocity.
   (Hint: what does a negative velocity mean physically?)

   -30m/5s = -6 m/s (moving in reverse)

3. A UE5 actor's Tick() runs with DeltaTime = 0.02 seconds and Velocity = 10 units/sec.
   How far does it move in that single tick?

   10 units/sec * 0.02 sec = 0.2 units

4. Same actor as Q3, but the game hitches and DeltaTime = 0.25 for one frame instead
   of the usual ~0.016. How far does it move in that one tick? What visible problem
   could this cause in a fast-moving object (e.g. a bullet)?

   10 u/s * 0.25 s = 2.5 units (this can cause the projectile to suddenly speed up for a tick causing breaking coherence)
   //Added correction: The sudden large increment can cause it to skip the wall so from before the wall and land past it creating collision problems

5. Position samples: at t=1s, x=4m. At t=3s, x=16m. Compute the average velocity
   between these two samples.

   12m/2s = 6 m/s

6. Conceptual: explain in your own words why "average rate of change over 4 seconds"
   is NOT the same thing as "instantaneous velocity at t=2 seconds." Give a concrete
   scenario where they'd clearly differ (e.g. a car that speeds up partway through).

   So a punch for example accelerates as it goes but a snap at the end would have a faster instantaneous velocity compared to right before
   Average is over the entire time between the two positions as instantaneous is the velocity at a specific time
   //Added correction: Average blends the slow and fast parts of the movement into a single average, while instantaneous velocity only cares about the value at one specific moment

7. Conceptual: why does multiplying movement by DeltaTime (instead of a fixed
   constant like 1) make a game's movement framerate-independent? What would go
   wrong on a 30fps machine vs a 144fps machine if you used a fixed constant instead?

   The velocity would be different DelatTime calculates the necessary number to create the velocity we need at each frame, making the game framerate-independent
   //Added correction: A 30fps machine would be at 150units/sec as 144fps would be 720units/sec without DeltaTime calculating the necessary number on each frame. Like so DeltaTime will adjust to keep both results to 5(or near enough) so is necessary for framerate-independance

8. A velocity-from-position calculation divides by Δtime. Under what real
   circumstance could Δtime be exactly 0, and what must the code do to stay safe?

   Right at the start of the movement, so we must have a DeltaTime>small epsilon (0.001f or a value that is judged large enough to be safe)
